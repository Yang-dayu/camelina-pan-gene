Reading the results

What each column, badge and state means
Annotation names
DH55V2 and DH55V3 are two annotations of DH55. SunesonDecker2025 (Montana State University, MSU_CS-sun), SunesonJGI, SunesonFang2023 and SunesonBrock2024 are four annotations of Suneson. Downloaded tables and the dataset files use the data ID Suneson for SunesonDecker2025.
Homeologs
One row per annotation, one column per subgenome. The highlighted column is the query's own subgenome: the gene that corresponds to the query in that annotation (same chromosome number), with its evidence. The other two columns are its homeologs on the other subgenomes. Top hit under an annotation name marks a strongest protein match that is none of the three, for example a copy on another chromosome or an unplaced sequence.
Evidence
Ranked hits lists the best protein hits in each annotation by bit score; Related genes lists every syntenic or OrthoFinder partner, whatever its rank. The strongest sequence match is not necessarily the corresponding gene: in a hexaploid, a copy on another subgenome or a partial match can score higher than the syntenic copy.
synteny anchor
Two genes in conserved gene order on the same-numbered chromosome, from GENESPACE. Anchor: a pair used to build the syntenic block. Open details for its source run and block.
cross-chromosome synteny
Two genes in conserved gene order on different-numbered chromosomes, from the all-chromosome run. They may be subgenome copies or a moved region; this alone does not show which.
OrthoFinder
OrthoFinder lists these two genes as orthologs.
same entry
Both genes are in the same pan-gene entry, with the query annotation as the reference. This does not mean they are directly syntenic.
partial
One of the two coverages is below 50%: a local match. With high identity this calls for checking the annotation structure; it is not by itself evidence of a split gene.
Rank
Order by bit score among every candidate stored for that annotation (the self hit is listed separately). Each search reports at most 25 targets, so no hit does not prove absence.
Subgenome copies
  • Camelina sativa is allohexaploid: most genes have three copies, one per subgenome (SG1, SG2, SG3; names as in Chaudhary et al. 2020, groups as in Martin et al. 2022).
  • The highlighted column shows the corresponding gene in each annotation (same chromosome number).
  • The other two columns show predicted homeologs: the best protein match on the chromosome where that copy is expected.
  • Each cell states its evidence (synteny + OrthoFinder, cross-checked, sequence only, found via homeolog) and how many near-equal candidates exist.
  • check means this pick deserves a second look, for example another gene may be better, or a near-identical homeolog has no second check. Open its details to see why.
  • Not found under current rules can mean the copy is on an unplaced sequence, has no protein model, falls below the thresholds, or is absent. The cell's details say which.
to verify
Other annotations suggest this gene belongs on another chromosome, or it lies in a region flagged for that reason. It keeps its original position. Its candidates have no confirmed subgenome, so do not read them as established corresponding genes or homeologs.
Why it is flagged
  • This gene: among other annotations that have a syntenic partner for it, most place that partner on a different chromosome.
  • Nearby genes: many genes around it point to another chromosome, so it is flagged with them.
  • Either flag asks for a check. It does not prove that a gene moved or that an assembly is wrong.

Three subgenome copies

Predicted SG1, SG2 and SG3 copies in every annotation. Genes whose position conflicts with the evidence are flagged to verify, never silently reassigned.
