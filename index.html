Reading the results

Best sequence hit
The highest-scoring protein hit in that genome. It is the strongest sequence match, not necessarily the corresponding gene: a partial or a cross-chromosome match can score higher than the syntenic copy. Open a row to see all hits and the related genes.
synteny anchor
Direct syntenic pair from GENESPACE (same-numbered chromosomes only); anchor means the pair anchors its block.
OrthoFinder
The two genes sit on opposite sides of an OrthoFinder pairwise orthologue row.
same entry
Both genes are in the same pan-gene entry of the query genome's frame. Shared membership is not direct synteny.
partial
One of the two coverages is below 50%: a local match. With high identity this calls for checking the annotation structure; it is not by itself evidence of a split gene.
Rank
Order by bit score among every candidate stored for that genome (the self hit is listed separately). Each search reports at most 25 targets, so no hit does not prove absence.
Homeologs
Camelina sativa is allohexaploid: most genes have three copies, one per subgenome (SG1, SG2, SG3; Chaudhary et al. 2020). The column of the query's own subgenome shows its ortholog in each genome (same chromosome number); the other two columns show predicted homeologs — the best protein match on that subgenome's chromosome of the query's homeolog group. Each cell states its evidence (synteny + OrthoFinder, two routes agree, sequence only, via own homeolog) and how many near-equal candidates exist. check marks a pick that another candidate beats on a different criterion (for example a near-identical fragment next to the full-length pick), or a near-identical homeolog that nothing confirms; open its details to see why. Not found under current rules can mean the copy sits on an unplaced sequence, has no protein model, falls below the thresholds, or is absent; the cell's details say which.
